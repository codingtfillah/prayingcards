Praying with Playing Cards

Explore Activities Get the Deck
The Project

Playing with the words of t'fillah is a way of praying

Created by Oren Kaunfer, Madrich Ruchani at JCDS Boston, Praying Cards is an educational tool that helps middle schoolers build a personal, spiritual, and emotional connection to the language of Jewish prayer.

The deck is built around the 36 most commonly used Hebrew roots in the Shacharit service — words like אהב (love), אור (light), אחד (oneness), and שלום (peace). Two of each root make a full deck of 72 cards.

Each card features an abstract watercolor illustration on the front, and on the back: the Hebrew root, multiple interpretive translations, liturgical examples, and thought-provoking reflective prompts. The format is tactile, scalable, and deeply playful.

How to Play

Suggested Activities

These games invite students to engage with the siddur through conversation, movement, creativity, and reflection.

🃏

Word Match

The leader reads a prompt — like "When I woke up this morning, I felt…" — and students choose the card that fits them best, then share why.

Whole Class
👑

T'fillah Headbands

Students place their word on their forehead and walk around asking peers: "What does this look like? Can you show it with a motion?"

Movement
🎯

Pick a Kavanah (T'fillah Bingo)

Each student draws a card as they enter the beit knesset. It becomes their focus word throughout t'fillah — they raise a hand each time it appears.

During Prayer
✍️

Build a Brachah

Small groups compose an original prayer using their assigned root words. Each group shares their creation with the class.

Creative Writing
🤝

Makom Kavanah

Each student selects a card, then finds a partner whose word they'd like to "borrow" as an additional kavanah for the day. The pair discusses what drew them together.

Partner Work
🔄

Colander of T'fillah

In three rounds, partners get their teammate to guess words — first with any clue, then with one word, then with only motion. The ultimate prayer charades!

Game
🎨

What Does This Look Like?

With the image side down, students describe their own imagined illustration. Then they flip the card and compare — surprise and discussion follow.

Visual Reflection
🖌️

In My Image

In art class, students design their own artwork for a resonant word. Partners then guess the inspiration in chevruta.

Art + Chevruta
➕

Tovim HaShnayim

Students select one card, then create a second card using a root word of their own choosing — from the siddur or from life. They combine both for a unique kavanah.
